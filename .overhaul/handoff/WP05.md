**What the lens does.** Every selection change (a tap, the back stack, a deep link, a drag's release) moves the lens on
its own settle spring, and that spring is the only input: no new animation, no new coroutine.
`travel = min(s(min(1, √(v² + k·d²) / 6 slots/s)), lift)`, `s` the smoothstep. The first term is the spring's energy,
expressed as the top speed it could still give the lens (`v` its speed, `d` the distance left to its target, `k` the
spring's stiffness); the smoothstep lets the glass calm with the motion. `lift` is the glass the last frame showed:
each frame raises it by at most `min(Δx / 0.2 slot, Δt / 32 ms)` (`Δx` how far the lens moved in that frame, `Δt` the
frame's time) and caps it at the energy's glass. So the glass materializes over the first fifth of a tab, never in
fewer than four 120 Hz frames, and a new selection, which raises the energy at once, starts from the glass on screen
instead of jumping to the new energy's. `lift` is snapshot state written only by the frame loop (its step, and the
jump with animations off, which clears it); it is held at 0 while the lens does not refract, so glass that returns
mid-flight also rises from plain. Travel is 0 on the frame of a tap (the lens has not moved yet) and always 0 without
`refracts`. `intensity = max(pressure, travel)`. Per frame, read only in the lens's own layer block and draw:
- layer scale (`LiquidLensState.scale(orientation)`): swell `1 + max(0.3 · pressure, 0.12 · travel)`, stretched along
  the orientation by `1 + 0.2 · travel` and narrowed across by `1 − 0.08 · travel`; never below 1 on either axis. The
  stretch follows travel alone: a press held still swells evenly, with nothing to stretch along;
- `onDrawBackdrop`: the sample magnified `1 + 0.12 · intensity` about the lens's centre (a canvas transform, no pass);
- `onDrawSurface`: the resting selection colour at alpha `1 − intensity`, so the refraction shows through as it fades;
- the effect chain is the static `LensEffects` (vibrancy, blur 2 dp, lens 12/20 dp): no dispersion, no inner shadow,
  no highlight, no shadow, built once.

`samplesBackdrop` (pressed where the lens `refracts`, or a flag the frame loop writes at the end of each frame:
`intensity >= 0.01`) gates everything. While it is false the lens's layer alpha is 0 (HWUI skips the node, no offscreen
pass), its draw skips the sample, and its layer and draw read nothing else; an unchanged flag notifies no one, so the
frames the spring still takes to settle after the hand-over invalidate nothing. `refracts` is
`GlassQuality.Live && !reduceMotion`; without it `pressure` and `travel` stay 0, so the same state drives a plain
indicator.

Between selections the spring's energy only drains (`dE/dt = −2ζ√k · v²`), so the glass lifts once, calms once and
holds through any turnaround: a selection reversed in flight (a tap and a tap back, a tap and Back) and an overshoot. A
second selection made at any frame of the first lifts the glass from where it is and no faster than a tap from rest.
Timings on the `thumb` spring (response 0.28 s, bounce 0, so k 504 and ζ 1 in all four motion styles) at 120 Hz,
computed from Compose's spring closed form with the loop's whole-millisecond steps (`$O/lens_timing.py`, output in

**Decisions (runs 4 to 12, the user away).**
- The refraction shader's height and amount stay fixed and `effects` reads no state: Kyant rebuilds the whole chain on
  any state it saw change there (`DrawBackdropNode.observeEffects`), and `RenderEffect.createRuntimeShaderEffect`
  copies the uniforms, so a per-frame refraction amount means a new native effect chain every frame. The refraction
  the eye sees still follows `intensity`, in draw: the resting tint fades off the lens (`1 − intensity`) and the sample
  magnifies (`1 + 0.12 · intensity`).
- Reduce motion (animator scale 0) keeps the lens plain for taps, programmatic selection and drags alike: no swell,
  stretch, magnification or refraction; the indicator still slides and follows the finger. Run 5 overruled run 4,
  which had kept the swell and refraction under a finger as direct manipulation, because three sources say otherwise:
  the orchestrator's brief ("Reduce motion: no distortion"), `research/ios-motion-catalogue.md` (Reduce Motion turns
  elastic glass off; the interactive press is "Off under ReduceMotion", Apple S18), and the design system's own
  `Modifier.pressScale`, which does nothing under reduce motion. If refraction stops while a finger is down (a tier
  drop mid-drag), the swell recedes on the press spring instead of vanishing.
- **Travel is the spring's energy, not its speed** (run 8, kept by runs 9 and 10). Read off the speed alone (run 7),
  the glass dropped to the plain indicator for one or two frames whenever a selection turned the lens around in
  flight: the speed passes through zero while the lens is still far from its new tab. That is the blink that had made
  run 6 refuse overshooting springs, and a tap followed by Back hits it with any spring. The energy holds at a
  turnaround and costs one square root per read. It departs from the brief's wording ("from the lens's own slot spring
  velocity") but not its intent: it reads only the lens's own spring and adds no animation or coroutine. Run 6's
  `require(dampingRatio >= 1)` went with it: an overshoot no longer blinks, so a bouncy thumb token gives an
  overshooting droplet instead of an exception at the bar's first composition.
- **The glass materializes over the first fifth of a tab** (run 10, overruling run 9). Runs 8 and 9 let a tap lift
  the lens in one frame: the energy at the start of any tap (√k per slot, 22 slots/s on the thumb spring) is well past
  the 6 slots/s at which travel is full, so the smoothstep never eased the lift, and the lens went from the plain
  indicator to 1.12 swell, 1.2 stretch and no tint in one 120 Hz frame. Run 9 had kept that as "instantly flexing"
  (catalogue, Interactive press), but that line describes a finger's press; for glass appearing, the catalogue's
  Materialize row says it "materializes in and out by gradually modulating the light bending and lensing", and the
  brief asks for an intensity "normalised and clamped, eased". Raising `TravelFullSpeed` could not help (the energy is
  highest at the start, so a higher value only weakens and shortens the glass), so a lift read off the distance the
  lens covers eases it in, which still needs no animation or coroutine.

## Deviations from MASTER-PLAN, with evidence

- **D1. Kyant #100 is the recording cycle, not distance.** The issue (github.com/Kyant0/AndroidLiquidGlass/issues/100,
  read 2026-10-10): a `Scaffold` box with `layerBackdrop(contentBackdrop)` holds the pager and the screens; the bottom
  bar outside it samples fine; Screen3's top bar, *inside* that box, samples `contentBackdrop` and the app crashes. The
  maintainer's answer: the top bar must be outside `contentBackdrop`. In the 2.0.1 source a `LayerBackdropNode` records
  its whole subtree (`backdrops/LayerBackdropModifier.kt:55-58`), so a `DrawBackdropNode` inside it records a layer
  that contains itself, which HWUI cannot draw (SIGSEGV on the RenderThread). Nothing in the source depends on how far
  apart the samplers are: a sampler outside the recorded subtree only references the layer, which is what
  `rememberGlassChrome` relies on for the bar group (to be confirmed on the phone by the step 7 journey).
  Consequences: (a) `SceneBackdrop.drawBackdrop` walks the sampler's `parentCoordinates` (Compose 1.12:
  `NodeCoordinator.parentCoordinates` is `wrappedBy`, which visits every coordinator and crosses into the parent's
  inner coordinator, where the scene node sits) and throws `IllegalStateException` naming the fix when the sampler is
  inside the scene (tested), instead of a native crash; (b) a sampler in another window (dialog, popup, Kyant #91)
  draws only the background; (c) **top-bar buttons stay drawn glass**: they live inside each screen, which is inside
  the recorded content, so they cannot sample the stage's scene, and an exported backdrop per screen would add a
  second recording and a second blur for 44 dp buttons. The benchmark journey of step 7 still runs with the bar and
  top-bar actions on screen together.
- **D2. The window background goes into each sample, not into the recording.** A4.9 rule 1 paints it into the
  recording; `SceneBackdrop` fills each sample's own layer (`Canvas.drawColor`) before drawing the scene into it. Same
  result at the edges (the blur never pulls in transparency), but the fill covers the bar's area (~0.15 MP on the
  Redmi) instead of the whole window (2.6 MP) every frame.
- **D3. The person's own choices are reversible within a session.** A4.9 says tiers never upgrade within a session.
  Reduce transparency and battery saver take effect both ways at once (a setting that sticks until restart reads as
  broken); the measured signals ratchet: the hottest thermal status seen this session counts (`rememberSaveable`, so
  it survives rotation) and the GPU verdict only ever flips to bad. That keeps the anti-flapping intent.
- **D4. The verdict is keyed by fingerprint + install time, not version code.** `PackageInfo.lastUpdateTime` changes on
  every install and update; a version code does not change when a developer reinstalls a fix under the same code, so a
  stale "bad" verdict would keep their device frosted while they measure. `perf.sh`'s `pm clear` wipes it anyway.
- **D5. No second scroll-edge fade.** WP03's `ds/foundation/scroll/ScrollEdge.kt` (`scrollEdgeFade`, unmerged) is
  periflow's G11 sketch exactly: one recording per frame, a strip in an offscreen `GraphicsLayer` masked with
  `BlendMode.DstIn`, no `RenderEffect`, so it works on every API. The Glass style uses it under a clear top bar
  (step 8); a copy in `foundation/glass` would be dead weight at the merge. Verify it on API 30 (emulator) when the
  style lands.
- **D6. AGSL cannot be built off the main thread with Kyant 2.0.1.** Each `DrawBackdropNode` owns a
  `RuntimeShaderCacheImpl` (`RuntimeShaderCache.kt:10-21`, sealed interface, `BackdropEffectScope.kt:32`) and builds
  `RuntimeShader(String)` on first use inside `updateEffects()`, on the main thread; a shader made elsewhere cannot be
  handed in. Mitigation (S6): the bar node lives as long as the shell, the lens node stays attached at rest, and WP04's
  idle warm-up pass draws the glass once. Only `lens()` builds a shader: `highlight = null` everywhere.
- **D7. AD-40's "frosted during a live resize" is not built.** Switching tier detaches Kyant's nodes (their layers are
  released and their shader caches cleared in `onDetach`) and attaches new ones, which is likely dearer than the
  per-frame reallocation it avoids. It needs a measurement on a resizable window first.
- **D9. SHEET-SPEC §11.5 item 5, as written, would change nothing; the scene now tracks no position at all** (run
  14, `f11af5d`). The item asks `SceneBackdrop` to invalidate samples on `scene.localPositionOf(surface)` instead of
  `positionInRoot()`, so the card stack's shared transform re-samples neither the bar nor a Glass sheet. One scene
  serves several surfaces, so there is no single offset to key a shared state on, and the invalidation that decides is
  Kyant's own: `DrawBackdropNode` keeps its surface's coordinates in `mutableStateOf(null, neverEqualPolicy())`, writes
  them in every `onGloballyPositioned` while the backdrop `isCoordinatesDependent`, and reads them while it records the
  sample (`$OV/scratch/kyant-backdrop/commonMain/com/kyant/backdrop/DrawBackdropModifier.kt`, `onGloballyPositioned`
  and `recordBackdropBlock`). Compose 1.12 sends that callback to every position-aware node under a layer whose
  position changes (`NodeCoordinator.updateLayerParameters` calls `requestOnPositionedCallback` when the layer's
  positional properties change and its subtree has observers, and `OnPositionedDispatcher.dispatchHierarchy` walks the
  whole subtree: `$OV/out/WP03/src/ui/commonMain/androidx/compose/ui/node/NodeCoordinator.kt:612-677`,
  `OnPositionedDispatcher.kt:78-85`). So under a shared transform the bar's sample is recorded again whatever the
  scene keys on, and a Glass sheet outside the presenter has to re-sample anyway, its offset and scale to the scene
  having changed. Run 14 removed the scene's own share instead: the recording node no longer listens for positions
  (a callback into the content box, a walk to the root, a state write and an invalidation of every sample on each
  frame of such a transform); a sample asks it for its coordinates while it draws, as the jetpack-compose-performance
  skill prescribes for `Modifier.Node`. The rest needs a sample per surface ("What remains" 0), which changes every
  caller, `wp/WP05-glass`'s `LiquidTabs` among them, so it waits for that merge.
- **D8. The GPU budget is the refresh period, not the frame deadline.** A4.9 says "exceeds the refresh budget"; the
  code uses one refresh period per frame: the GPU renders one frame at a time, so a GPU that needs more than a period
  per frame falls further behind every frame even when the pipeline gives each frame a deadline two periods away.

- Only floating chrome in `GlassStage`'s `chrome` slot samples the scene: the tab bar, its accessory, its trailing
  capsule. Everything inside a screen (cards, chips, fields, top-bar buttons, sheets) is `Modifier.glassSurface`, which
  samples nothing. A sampling surface inside the recorded content fails with `IllegalStateException` (it would sample
  itself: Kyant #100, a native RenderThread crash). Dialogs and popups are separate windows: drawn glass only (#91).
- Below API 31 Kyant draws the backdrop sharp, so those devices are `Frosted`; the lens is AGSL and needs API 33, and
  throws on any shape that is not a rounded rectangle (`liquidLens` takes `RoundedRectangularShape` for that reason).
- Never read state inside Kyant's `effects` (it rebuilds the RenderEffect chain on every change): effects are
  top-level values. Animate glass through `layerBlock` (scale) and `onDrawSurface` (tint alpha).
- A tab tap never presses the lens (PeriFlow G1: the press re-rendered the whole bar with inner shadow and dispersion
  for about 0.5 s). The tap's glass comes from the lens's own spring: its energy (speed and distance left), which a
  lift raises the glass towards a little each frame as the lens moves (`LiquidLensState.travel`), read only in the
  lens element's layer block and draw; the bar body, the tabs and composition never read it. Only `Live` lifts the lens, and under Reduce
  motion it is never glass, for a tap or a drag.
- Kyant 2.0.1 records a sampling surface's sample again, and blurs it again, on every frame in which the surface's
  position on screen changes, through a layer on any ancestor too (a shell slide, a card-stack scale, a press swell
  around the bar). A transform the stage shares with its chrome changes nothing the sample shows, but it is not free
  until the per-surface sample lands ("What remains" 0). The scene itself reads its position only while a sample
  draws; never track coordinates in state for glass (`onGloballyPositioned` into `mutableStateOf`).
- Never put an alpha < 1 layer, `AnimatedVisibility` fade or `saveLayer` with alpha around a sampling surface: hide by
  translation and `alpha = 0f` only when fully hidden. Glass appears by refraction or tint, never by layer alpha.
- Build a `drawBackdrop` chain once (`remember`): Kyant compares its per-call shape provider by identity, so a chain
  rebuilt each recomposition rebuilds its effects and re-renders the backdrop.
- `highlight = null`, `shadow = null` on every Kyant node; glass shadows are `glassSurface`'s cached outer-only shadow,
  drawn outside the sampled layer, and none in dark theme.
- Content glass under a fading ancestor: fade with `CompositingStrategy.ModulateAlpha`, or the outer shadow is cut off
  square by the offscreen layer.
- Nothing inside the recorded scene animates forever (it re-blurs every sample every frame): ambient animations stop on
  `LocalTabVisible == false`.
- Tiers: `rememberGlassQuality` decides from API level, low RAM, battery saver, thermal status (the hottest seen this
  session), Reduce transparency (default: the system's high-contrast text on API 36+) and a GPU verdict (two
  consecutive 120-frame windows in which more than 15 % of frames took the GPU longer than one refresh period, kept
  per system build and install). Force one with the dev extra `glass.quality=<Tier>`; measure only release builds with
  `perf.sh`.
